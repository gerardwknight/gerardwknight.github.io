Dastardly Words Privacy Policy

Effective 13 October 2026

Dastardly Words is a word puzzle game made by Gerard Knight, a sole developer in Victoria, Australia. This page explains what information the game collects, why, and who it is shared with. The short version: there are no accounts, we never ask for your name or email address, and the game works without telling us who you are.

What stays on your device

Your puzzle progress, streaks, statistics and settings are saved on your device. They are not sent to us. If you delete the game, they are deleted with it.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The free version shows video ads between some puzzles, and you can choose to watch a video in exchange for help with a puzzle. These ads are provided by Google AdMob. To show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, AdMob collects information such as your device's advertising identifier, approximate location derived from your IP address, and information about how ads are viewed and interacted with.

On iPhone, the game asks your permission before it can use your device's advertising identifier for tracking. If you say no, you will still see ads, but they won't be personalised using that identifier. You can change your answer at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Google's use of this information is covered by Google's Privacy Policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Purchases (Apple and RevenueCat)

If you buy the in-app purchase, payment is handled entirely by Apple. We never see your card details or your Apple ID. We use RevenueCat to confirm the purchase and restore it on your other devices. RevenueCat receives a random identifier created by the app, your purchase history for this game, and basic device information. See RevenueCat's Privacy Policy.

Game analytics (PostHog)

To check whether puzzles are labelled with the right difficulty, and to see where new players get stuck, the game sends a small number of anonymous gameplay events to PostHog, hosted in the European Union. For example: a puzzle was finished, how many tries and helps it took, and whether a help video was watched.

  • Events are tied only to a random install identifier created by the app. It is not linked to your name, email, Apple ID or advertising identifier.
  • Your IP address is used only to work out your country and then discarded. It is not stored.
  • We don't record your screen, your typing, or anything outside the game.
  • This data is kept for up to one year.

See PostHog's Privacy Policy.

Game settings file

The game may download a small settings file from GitHub to switch features on or off without an update. Like any website, GitHub receives your IP address when the file is downloaded. No information about you or your play is sent with that request.

If you email us

If you contact us by email, we'll receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to reply, and we don't add you to any mailing list.

Children

Dastardly Words is made for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, contact us and we'll delete it.

Your choices and rights

  • You can refuse ad tracking when asked, or change it later in your device settings.
  • Because we don't know who you are, we can't look up your analytics by name. Deleting the game resets the random install identifier, so new events can't be connected to old ones.
  • If you'd like to ask about, correct, or delete information we hold, or make a complaint, email us. We handle requests under the Australian Privacy Principles. If you're not satisfied with our answer, you can contact the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

We don't sell your information

We don't sell your personal information to anyone. The only services that receive data from the game are the ones named on this page, for the purposes described.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update it here and change the date at the top. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the game's update notes.
